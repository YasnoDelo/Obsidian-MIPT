
Imagine a scene in a detective film. A woman tells her son, “I believe you.” He leaves the room. We hear a drawer close, and the scene ends. One detail changes the meaning of her words. As soon as he turns away, she locks his passport in the drawer. If you miss that gesture, you have a very different question to discuss after the film. You may wonder where the son is going. The person beside you may wonder whether he can trust his mother.  

This is why I want to start Open Frame, a non-profit cinema club for blind and visually impaired film lovers. Our club will offer free monthly screenings with audio description, followed by discussions open to members, their friends and anyone who enjoys talking about films.  
**Our mission is to bring blind and visually impaired film lovers together through accessible screenings. We put members in charge of choosing films and leading the conversations that follow.**  

Two values guide our work. The first is **independence**. In the scene I described, a voice could say, “She locks his passport in the drawer.” Those few words give the listener the same clue as the rest of the audience. Each person can then decide what the gesture means. Is the mother trying to protect her son? Is she trying to control him? A good description leaves that question open. 

Our second value is **participation**. In order to build a programme that reflects our members’ interests, we will invite them to choose each month’s film. We will work with a local cinema to test the audio equipment before every screening. Afterwards, a member will open the discussion with a question of their own. This matters to me because some of my favourite moments at a film club come when someone notices a detail that changes how I understand the whole story. Everyone deserves the chance to be that person.  

Picture our city ten years from now. **Blind and visually impaired residents can choose a film at any local cinema and join their friends at the same screening. Across the city, they host film clubs, introduce festivals and help decide which stories reach the screen.**  
For me, success sounds like a phone call on a Friday evening. A friend rings and asks, “What would you like to see tonight?” Every film on the programme is a possible answer.
